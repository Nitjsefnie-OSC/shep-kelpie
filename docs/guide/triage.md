# Triage

Kelpie can look at the issues other people open. It finds the ones nobody has touched, researches each against the repo, comments what it found and leaves it `needs-triage` for you. For an issue you opened yourself and is sure of, it goes one step further: it rewrites the body and marks the issue `ready-for-agent`.

```toml
[app.dogs.kelpie.triage]
pick = "maintainer"
```

- `pick` says whose issues. `"off"`, the default, is no one's. `"maintainer"` is the ones you open (`git.maintainer`, else the login `gh` acts as, compared ignoring case), and `"all"` is everyone's. Any other word stops the runner
- `wait_days` (default 7) is how many whole days kelpie waits on an author's word before it leaves the issue to you, and `max_questions` (1 to 10, default 10) is the most questions it may ask about one issue
- Four limits keep a burst of issues or one noisy author from spending the triager's budget: `per_day` (default 10) is the most triage calls in any 24 hours, `per_author_per_day` (default 2) the most on one author's issues in any 24 hours, `min_account_days` (default 30) the youngest an author's account may be, and `max_body_kb` (default 20) the most of an issue body the call is given. Each `0` turns that limit off
- `agent` (default `triager`) names the agent file that researches an issue. Its role must be `triager`. `add` writes `triager.md`, and you can edit it like any agent file
- Triage speaks as the App, so a `pick` other than `"off"` needs the GitHub App on the repo. The runner refuses to start without it and names `triage.pick`. A missing or wrong-role `triage.agent` or `agents.representative` is refused the same way
- Only an issue opened after you turned `pick` on is looked at, so an old backlog is left alone. Kelpie keeps that moment (`triage.since`) in the project's state, forgets it when you start with `pick = "off"`, and takes a new one when you turn it on again
- Once a minute the runner lists the open issues. An issue is untouched when it carries none of `needs-triage`, `needs-info`, `ready-for-agent`, `ready-for-human`, `wontfix` and `possible-spam`, a bot did not open it (the App included), and it was opened after the moment above
- `shep kelpie status` shows the untouched ones, oldest first, as `triage due`. The JSON has them under `triage.due`, and leaves the key out when none is due. A listing that fails changes nothing and costs no poll
- The first pass runs when the board is open and a slot is free, after a contribution call and before the board's pick, one issue at a time. The triager reads a clean view of `origin/main` with no shell and no plugins, and has 15 minutes
- Before that call the four limits are checked, in that order, and none of them calls a model. Your own issues and one `shep kelpie triage` asked for skip all four.
  - Past `per_day` the issue waits in `triage due` for the next free place, and is never dropped. The count is of every triager call that reached a model, saved the moment the call ends, so it survives a restart even on an issue that has no record yet, one issue can spend more than one place of it, and a call whose reply could not be read spends a place like any other. A cap above 200 is refused: kelpie keeps the last 200 finished triage records, a record that holds a call ahead of one that holds none, which is all a 24 hour count can see
  - Past `per_author_per_day` the issue gets `needs-triage` with no call and no comment, and the record says the limit held it. Logins are compared ignoring case
  - Under `min_account_days`, and with no history in the repo (GitHub counts the author as `NONE`, `FIRST_TIMER` or `FIRST_TIME_CONTRIBUTOR`), the same hold. The account's age is read once per author per start, and one kelpie cannot read is held by nothing
  - Past `max_body_kb` the body reaches the call cut at that size, with a line in the fence saying it was cut and how long the whole body was. The title is never cut
  - A `needs-triage` from a limit is final for that issue: the pass does not come back to it, and `shep kelpie triage <n>` is how you have it triaged anyway
  - The author's round is gated by the day's cap as the first pass is, since its calls spend the same budget. A round call with no free place is left for the next poll, and is never labelled over: the issue is already triaged. The author's cap and the account check decide which of an author's issues kelpie takes on, so they reach only an issue kelpie has not called on yet: a call that came to nothing cannot end an issue for good. An issue whose author's account was deleted since it went up is held by no author cap, since it has no login to count. A record that names no author, which is what a file saved before these limits existed leaves, gets it from the listing at the next poll, so your own round is not held by a limit it should skip
- With `[app.dogs.kelpie.triage.spam]` set, and no table means no check, an issue the limits leave alone then goes to a text classifier a gateway serves, before its research call. `gateway` names a gateway from kelpie's own `[gateways.<name>]` settings, `path` is the classifier's route on it, such as `/classify`, and `threshold` (default 0.8) is the probability at or above which the issue is read as spam. The classifier is given the issue's title, its body as the call is given it, the author's association and how many days the author's account has existed, and is asked one question: is this issue spam, an advertisement, or text unrelated to the project?
  - At or above `threshold` the issue gets `possible-spam`, made if the repo lacks it, and nothing else: no comment, no research call, no close, no lock and no hide. The label is the ask, and you decide. `shep kelpie triage <n>` is how you have it triaged anyway, and it takes `possible-spam` off, since asking for the issue is your answer to the read. A `possible-spam` you put on by hand stays, as any hand label does. `status` says the issue was read as spam and with what probability
  - Below `threshold` the pass goes on as it was
  - Your own issues and one `shep kelpie triage` asked for skip the check, as they skip the limits
  - A `gateway` kelpie's settings do not have, or one it cannot dial, keeps the runner from starting and names `triage.spam.gateway`. A gateway that cannot be reached, one that refuses the request, or one whose key kelpie's environment does not hold, is told once and the check is off for the rest of the run, with the limits still holding, so an outage opens no flood. The next start asks it again. An answer kelpie cannot read is told once and leaves the check on, so the next issue is still asked and that one goes on to its research call
  - Each check that answered is one line in the call ledger as role `triager`, kind `spam-check`, with the issue, no tokens and no cost. It is not a triager call: it spends no place of `per_day` or `per_author_per_day`, and adds nothing to the `triager` total in `shep kelpie usage`
- It comments once as the App, in the representative's voice, with the files, tests, related issues, draft criteria and any questions only the author can answer. A marker under the comment lets kelpie find it again. Then it puts the type (`bug` or `enhancement`) and priority on the issue if the repo has that label, a priority under the board's name `priority: P2` first and under the bare `P2` where your repo names them that way, then `needs-triage`, made if missing. A `P0` is never set: it comes up as a question
- When the pass has a question only the author can answer, and the author is one kelpie asks (you, on an issue you opened, or anyone at all under `pick = "all"`), that comment ends asking them how to settle it: reply `github` and kelpie asks the questions on GitHub, reply `local` and kelpie hands them one prompt to settle the questions in an agent session of their own, reply `none` and you triage the issue as it stands. While it waits for the word, no status label goes on: the type and priority wait until the round ends. `none`, a reply kelpie can read as neither word twice over, or no word within `wait_days` puts the labels on, leaves the issue `needs-triage` and posts nothing more, so the first comment stays your starting point. A reply kelpie cannot read gets one plain comment asking once more
- `github` posts one comment with the questions numbered and each with the answer kelpie's research recommends, at most `max_questions` of them. The author answers them in one comment, and the triager reads the issue a second time with the questions and the answers. For an issue you opened, that pass ends the way a sure pass does, `ready-for-agent` included. For one someone else opened, what their answers settle goes under their own text in the issue body, never in place of it, and the issue ends `needs-triage` with what is still open listed in the comment. One round of questions is the whole of it: there is no second round, and no answers within `wait_days` ends it at `needs-triage`. A label you or someone else put on by hand, or closing the issue, ends the wait with no post and no label
- `local` posts one comment: a short lead-in and a prompt to paste into an agent session of the author's own, built from the `grill-with-docs` skill (mattpocock/skills; kelpie's own copy is at `skills/skills/engineering/grill-with-docs`). The prompt names the issue, tells the session to read it with `gh issue view <issue> --comments` and the files the triage comment named, lists the open questions with the answers kelpie's research recommends, and ends by having the session rewrite the issue body with acceptance criteria and a `Touches:` list and comment the decisions it made. Kelpie then reads the issue once a minute: a body that changed since the prompt went up is triaged again on its new text, and that second look ends the round as the answers round does, `ready-for-agent` for an issue you opened that the pass is sure of and `needs-triage` for any other, with what is still open in the comment and no further ask. No changed body within `wait_days` of the prompt ends at `needs-triage` with nothing posted, and a label put on by hand or a closed issue ends it with no post and no label. Kelpie's own rewrite of a body is never read as the author's edit
- An issue you opened yourself, and one the pass is sure of, goes further: sure means a type, a priority, no open question and a body the triager rewrote with acceptance criteria and a `Touches:` list of the files it read. Kelpie then replaces the issue's body with that rewrite (the title stays as you wrote it), puts the type and priority on, takes off the `needs-triage` an earlier pass left on, and puts `ready-for-agent` last, made if the repo lacks it, so the board can take the issue as any other. An issue someone else opened never goes past `needs-triage` on its own. Kelpie only says in the comment that it will do this where it can: a rewrite naming a path on this machine is dropped before the comment is worded and the issue ends at `needs-triage` with its body as you wrote it, and a rewrite your project's `publish` hook refuses is said on the issue in a short notice. A label you or someone else put on by hand stands, whenever it went on during the pass: kelpie adds nothing and leaves the body alone. A `ready-for-agent` kelpie itself already put on is not read as one you put on, and a restart finishes the labelling where it stopped
- The research also answers whether something already covers the issue. The triager is shown the open issues and the open pull requests, each pull request with the issues its body says it closes, and answers `addressed_by`: nothing, or the one open issue or pull request that does the same thing. When kelpie can check that the number is one it showed the triager, that it is open, and that it is not the issue itself, it posts one short comment naming it as `#n` with the reason, closes the issue as a duplicate, and puts no labels on it. A number it cannot check, or a cover whose kind it cannot read as an issue or a pull request, is dropped, told in the log, and the issue is triaged as any other
- An issue kelpie closed as a duplicate is not triaged again on its own if the author reopens it: its record keeps it out of the due list. `shep kelpie triage <n>` triages it again
- A call or a post that fails is told once in the log and tried at the next poll. Five failures give the issue up until a restart. A restart goes on from the step an issue got to and repeats no step it finished, so a body it already replaced is not written again, and a wait on an author keeps the time it began with. Beyond the author's round, only `shep kelpie triage` posts a second comment on an issue. An issue that already holds the App's marker comment is recorded with no call
- `shep kelpie triage <issue>` triages that issue next, past the `since` and author tests. It is refused when `pick` is `off`, when the issue is not open or already carries a triage label, or while its call is in flight. An issue kelpie already triaged is triaged again: a new comment is posted and replaces the record. An issue kelpie's classifier read as spam is triaged too, and its `possible-spam` comes off
- `shep kelpie status` shows the call in flight and each issue kelpie triaged as `triage` lines, naming an issue it closed as a duplicate and what it duplicates, and an issue a classifier read as spam with its probability. The JSON has them under `triage.running` and `triage.issues`, a closed one with its `duplicate_of` and a spam one with its `spam`. The day's count is a `triage daily: 7 of 10 used` line, with `next place in 4h` once the day is full, and the JSON has it under `triage.daily` as `used`, `cap`, `opens` and `wait`. An issue a limit held is named with the reason
- A change to the table waits for the next start, as `contributions` does

Reading an issue is the triager's work and reaches the repo read-only on every harness: Claude Code, pi, Codex and goose. Its calls total on a `triager` line in `shep kelpie usage`, apart from the work items.
